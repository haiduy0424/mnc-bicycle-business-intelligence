# Bicycle Retail Analytics: Sales Performance, Customer Segmentation & Demand Forecasting

An end-to-end business intelligence project analysing the sales performance, customer behaviour and regional markets of a multinational bicycle retailer (2017–2019), and forecasting 2020 sales under alternative business scenarios.

## 1. Project Objectives

- Assess sales, profitability and growth trends over 2017–2019
- Identify the products and markets driving growth
- Evaluate the impact of the shift from high-end to lower-priced product lines
- Compare market size, volatility, customer value and loyalty across regions
- Segment customers by purchasing behaviour and identify the most valuable groups
- Forecast 2020 sales to support planning decisions

## 2. Scope of Analysis

- **Sales Performance:** revenue, net profit, growth and product mix across Road, Mountain and Touring Bikes and Gears & Outfits
- **Product Portfolio:** volume, revenue and margin effects of divesting high-end models in favour of cheaper segments
- **Regional Markets:** North America, Europe and Australia, covering sales growth, volatility, demographics, customer value and loyalty
- **Customer Segmentation:** LRFM framework (Length, Recency, Frequency, Monetary) combined with K-Means clustering to group customers by loyalty, engagement and profitability
- **Cross-Selling:** detrended robust regressions testing whether Gears and Outfits sales support bike sales
- **Sales Forecasting:** ARIMA models for Gears & Outfits and VAR models for bikes, validated with diagnostic tests (serial correlation, heteroskedasticity, normality and stability)

## 3. Key Results and Insights

**Sales & Products**
- Sales reached **$16.35M in 2019** (+180% YoY) after a 17% decline in 2018, with net profit margin stable at ~30%
- Bikes generated 95% of sales; **Mountain Bikes** became the largest category (39%), overtaking Road Bikes
- **Touring Bikes**, launched in January 2019, grew ~14% per month on average and reached 23% of sales within a year
- The 2018 decline followed the divestment of the high-end Road Bike line: units sold rose 47%, but revenue fell $1.24M

**Product Strategy**
- The shift to cheaper segments **succeeded for Mountain Bikes** (sales $1.3M → $6.3M, with higher margins on lower-priced models)
- It **underperformed for Road Bikes**, which had not recovered to 2017 sales levels by 2019 and lost a higher-margin model

**Regional Markets**
- **US and Australia** were the largest markets in 2019 ($5.46M and $4.34M)
- **Europe** delivered the most consistent growth (2017–19 CAGR of 184–196% across UK, Germany and France)
- **North America** was the most volatile market, with sales falling 42–46% in 2018 before rebounding 250–280% in 2019

**Customers**
- **North America:** largest customer base (42% of customers) but the lowest average customer value ($1,211) and ~27% repeat purchases
- **Australia:** highest average customer value ($2,523) and strongest loyalty (72% repeat purchases), but limited new-customer acquisition
- **Europe:** moderate customer base and value ($1,623), with 33% repeat purchases
- In North America and Australia, the top loyal segments account for ~35–37% of customers but generate ~51–60% of revenue

**Cross-Selling & Forecast**
- 70–84% of bike buyers also purchased Gears, and Gears sales have a significant positive relationship with Mountain and Touring Bike sales, indicating cross-selling potential
- Gears & Outfits attracted new customers: 97% of new customers in 2019 bought these items
- The 2020 forecast models two scenarios: bike sales not resuming (5% likelihood) and bike sales resuming from February 2020 (95% likelihood)
